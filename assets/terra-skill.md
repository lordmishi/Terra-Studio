# Terra Studio: Documentary House Rules

General prehistory and natural history documentary channel template. Nothing is uploaded automatically; the creator handles editing and publishing directly.

## Audience
- Write for an older, curious documentary viewer: clear, confident, no slang, no hype words.
- Customize target demographics, audience location, and monetization notes in Terra Studio under **Settings -> YouTube & Channel -> Channel profile**.

## Topic rules
1. Lead with a **distinctive prehistoric predator or megafauna subject** matching your channel's top-performing topics.
2. Give it a clear hook: **a threat or rivalry angle**, or a **scale / record claim** backed by fossil evidence (flag debated claims explicitly in the script).
3. Avoid generic survey videos ("evolution of X") unless specifically requested; single-subject deep dives typically hold retention better.
4. **Never repeat an animal already published or already scheduled in the calendar.** Use `check_animal` and Terra Studio's calendar before proposing new topics.
5. Score each idea 0-5 across subject fit, predator/conflict tension, scale/record hook, evidence strength, and novelty. Suggest only 4-5.

## Title rules
- Under 60 characters. Name the animal, or make what it was immediately visual.
- One clear hook: threat, rivalry, record, or concrete number.
- Clickable yet honest. No "SHOCKING", no ALL CAPS clickbait, no question-only titles.
- Give 5 options, strongest first.

## Thumbnail rules
- One big focal subject, high contrast, readable at mobile feed size.
- 2-4 words of overlay text, uppercase, adding context rather than repeating the title.
- Natural, cinematic palette. No red arrows, no circles, no fake reaction faces.
- Generate 3 variants for YouTube Studio **Test & Compare**; upload and start tests manually in Studio.

## Script rules
- **Length: 3,000-5,000 words** (default 4,000 words, roughly 15 minutes at documentary narration pace).
- **5 acts.** Each act ends on an open question or forward pointer that the next act answers.
  1. Cold open: immediate scene and stakes in the first 15 seconds. No "welcome back", no "in this video", no early subscribe pitch.
  2. Context: size, era, habitat, with concrete numbers in digits.
  3. Behavior & mechanics: how it hunted, defended, or survived.
  4. Rivalry / human or ecosystem impact: honest about what is proven vs debated.
  5. Extinction & legacy: why it vanished and what it reveals. Optional brief subscribe line only at the very end.
- Give viewers an anchor (a number in digits, a size comparison, an action beat, or a question) at least every 80 words.
- Keep sentences short enough to read aloud (average under 22 words, none over 40).
- Never invent numbers, dates, or quotes. When a measurement is uncertain, state a range.

## Before upload (checklist)
- Facts: claims and figures match sources; debated hypotheses are framed carefully.
- Title under 60 characters and complements the thumbnail text.
- Description: first 2 lines open with the animal and hook; accurate chapter timestamps and sources included.
- Tags: common names, scientific genus, era, and core documentary keywords.
- Assets: 1280x720 thumbnail variants, accurate captions, calm end-screen last 20 seconds.

## After publishing
- Review CTR after 48 hours. If below 4%, test 3 fresh thumbnail concepts in YouTube Studio Test & Compare.
- Never alter published titles or thumbnails automatically.
